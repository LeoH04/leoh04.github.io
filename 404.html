Here is a small proof while you find your way back.

Suppose there are \(M\) models predicting the true value \(y_0\), and let \(\hat y_j\) be model \(j\)'s prediction.

\[c = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\hat y_j\]

Let the ensemble prediction be the average of the (M) model predictions:

\[c = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\hat y_j.\]

We begin with the average error of the individual models:

\[\operatorname{MSPE}_{\mathrm{individual}} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}(y_0-\hat y_j)^2.\]

Adding and subtracting the average prediction inside each squared error gives

\[\begin{aligned} \operatorname{MSPE}_{\mathrm{individual}} &= \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\left[(y_0-c)+(c-\hat y_j)\right]^2 \\ &= (y_0-c)^2 + \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}(c-\hat y_j)^2 + \frac{2(y_0-c)}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}(c-\hat y_j). \end{aligned}\]

The last term disappears because the deviations from their average sum to zero:

\[\sum_{j=1}^{M}(c-\hat y_j) = Mc-\sum_{j=1}^{M}\hat y_j = 0.\]

So the decomposition becomes

\[\operatorname{MSPE}_{\mathrm{individual}} = (y_0-c)^2 + \underbrace{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}(\hat y_j-c)^2}_{\text{diversity}}.\]

The first term is the squared error of the ensemble prediction. Rearranging therefore gives

\[\boxed{\operatorname{MSPE}_{\mathrm{ensemble}} = \operatorname{MSPE}_{\mathrm{individual}} - \text{diversity}}.\]

Therefore, holding individual-model MSPE fixed, more diversity means lower ensemble MSPE: this is the wisdom of the crowd.
